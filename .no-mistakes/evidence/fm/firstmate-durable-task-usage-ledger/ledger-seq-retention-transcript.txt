  seq=1 at=1788325865 event=ledger-open

  seq=3 at=1788325865 event=spawn

$ L prune --days 400

$ seqs
  seq=3 at=1745125865 event=ledger-open

(the coverage marker is the one record retention keeps; it carries the high-water mark)
$ seqs
  seq=3 at=1745125865 event=ledger-open
  seq=4 at=1788325865 event=spawn

-> next append is seq 4: neither 2 nor 3, the numbers the pruned records used.

[1mB. REGRESSED CLOCK - the condition the header now names.[0m
(the host clock jumps backwards, so the LAST appended record carries the OLDEST time)
$ seqs
  seq=1 at=1788325865 event=ledger-open
  seq=2 at=1788325865 event=spawn
  seq=3 at=1745125865 event=spawn
$ L prune --days 400
pruned 1 kept 2
$ seqs
  seq=1 at=1788325865 event=ledger-open
  seq=2 at=1788325865 event=spawn
$ seqs
  seq=1 at=1788325865 event=ledger-open
  seq=2 at=1788325865 event=spawn
  seq=3 at=1788325865 event=spawn

-> seq 3 is issued a second time, to a different event. Retention kept two
   dated records, so the marker carried nothing, and the last record kept
   was no longer the high-water mark. `id` stays unique either way:
$ sed -n 's/.*"seq":\([0-9]*\).*"id":"\([^"]*\)".*/  seq=\1 id=\2/p' "$(L path)"
  seq=1 id=ledger-open
  seq=2 id=spawn:gamma-x1:g1
  seq=3 id=spawn:delta-x1:g4

   This is precisely what the header now admits and previously claimed could
   not happen; `id`, the documented cross-export join key, is unaffected.
